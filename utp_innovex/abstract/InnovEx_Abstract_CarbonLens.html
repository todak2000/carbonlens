CarbonLens: A Browser-Native CO2 Geological Storage Screening & Simulation Studio

Daniel T. Olagunju · Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS
MSc research project · Open-source (MIT) · v0.1.0, released June 2026 · Live software demonstration available at the booth

Project Background

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) is central to Malaysia's decarbonisation pathway. The national CCUS Act 2025 (gazetted 1 October 2025) has established a licensing regime for CO2 storage, and multiple Malaysian storage complexes — including Kasawari, Duyong and the Malay Basin — are progressing toward development. Every such project begins with screening-level assessment: estimating storage capacity, predicting CO2 plume migration, bounding the pressure front (Area of Review), and checking geomechanical limits of the caprock before committing to expensive numerical simulation and field work.

Problem Statement

Early-stage CO2 storage assessment sits in an uncomfortable gap. Enterprise reservoir simulators are designed for generic multi-component oil-and-gas workflows: they are licence-heavy, require installation and specialist training, and are disproportionate for rapid screening. At the other extreme, spreadsheet calculators are transparent but oversimplified, and cannot propagate uncertainty or produce regulator-formatted outputs. Students, researchers and early-stage project developers lack a tool that is simultaneously rapid, rigorous, transparent, validated and free to run.

Proposed Solution

CarbonLens is a browser-native CO2 storage screening and simulation studio: the full physics engine runs client-side in any modern browser, with zero installation and zero backend. It implements peer-reviewed analytical and semi-analytical models — Span–Wagner and Peng–Robinson equations of state, Duan-family multi-salt solubility, Nordbotten vertical-equilibrium plume migration with Hesse post-injection gravity currents, Land/Killough hysteretic trapping, Theis pressure transients, DOE P10/P50/P90 capacity estimation, and Hubbert–Willis/Mohr–Coulomb geomechanical screening — inside a single integrated workspace of 16 panels, from fluid properties to a 3-D reservoir viewer, Monte Carlo uncertainty quantification, economics, leakage risk and regulator-ready export. Sixteen real formations worldwide are shipped as validated presets, including three Malaysian storage candidates.

Innovation

  • First browser-native full-physics CO2 storage studio. A multithreaded Web-Worker simulation engine with interactive 3-D plume and pressure visualisation replaces install-gated desktop software for the screening stage — assessment becomes a shareable web link.
  • White-box machine learning for CO2–brine interfacial tension. A MARS surrogate with inspectable, citable coefficients — externally validated at R² = 0.945 on a cross-laboratory holdout (RMSE < 2.1 mN/m) — with an applicability-domain gate (Green/Yellow/Red) that tells the user when the model should not be trusted, in contrast to black-box ML.
  • Calibrated uncertainty, everywhere. Monte Carlo propagation with conformal calibration carries P10/P50/P90 bounds through every reported quantity rather than single-point estimates.
  • Jurisdiction-matched permit pre-application generator. Twelve regulatory regimes — US EPA Class VI, UK NSTA, EU, Australia, Algeria, Nigeria, Indonesia, Egypt, UAE (MOEI), Alberta, and both Malaysian regimes (Federal CCUS Act 2025 and the separate Sarawak framework) — turn screening results into regulator-formatted pre-application figures in one click.
  • Verifiable assessment registry. Every exported assessment can carry a registry certificate (e.g. CL-XXXX-XX-XXXX) that third parties verify through a public route — a step toward auditable, shareable screening work.
