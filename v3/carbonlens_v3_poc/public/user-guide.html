CarbonLens User Guide

Complete step-by-step guide to pre-feasibility CO₂ storage simulation

Version 2.0 / July 2026 Daniel T. Olagunju, Founder & Lead Developer, CarbonLens™
Section 1

Introduction

CarbonLens is a browser-based CO₂ storage simulation studio that enables pre-feasibility analysis of geological storage sites without requiring any software installation. All computation runs locally in your browser using optimised JavaScript, with no data sent to any server.

The platform covers the complete pre-feasibility workflow from geological model construction through to permit-ready export packages, encompassing reservoir characterisation, physics-based fluid simulation, geomechanical safety assessment, economic analysis, and jurisdiction-specific regulatory compliance checking.

What CarbonLens is: A screening and pre-feasibility tool for CO₂ geological storage. Validated against 20 years of Sleipner monitoring data and 15 additional global benchmarks.

What CarbonLens is not: A replacement for Eclipse CO2STORE, CMG GEM, or TOUGH2/ECO2N for final-stage certification on committed injection projects. CarbonLens results should be followed by full-field simulation before injection permit approval on major projects.

Who This Guide Is For

  • CCUS engineers and consultants: Rapid multi-site screening, AoR delineation, permit pre-application packages
  • Academic researchers: Physics-based CO₂ storage teaching, sensitivity studies, MSc/PhD project support
  • Regulators and national energy bodies: Site screening for national CCS capacity assessments, regulatory pre-screening documentation
  • Petroleum engineers: Depleted field repurposing assessment, injectivity analysis, geomechanical risk screening
Section 2

Platform Architecture

CarbonLens runs two co-existing physics solvers that are automatically selected based on the simulation mode. Both run entirely in the browser without WebAssembly or server calls.

Table 1 - Physics Solvers
SolverTypeWhen usedReference
3D saturation solverExplicit FD (or IMPES)Grid View simulationPeaceman 1978, Land 1968
VE gravity current2D vertically integratedLong-timescale plume spreadNordbotten and Celia 2006

Core Physical Models

  • CO₂ density: Span-Wagner (1996) equation of state, precomputed as a 20x40 (T, P) lookup table with bilinear interpolation
  • CO₂ viscosity: Fenghour et al. (1998), co-tabulated with density
  • CO₂ solubility in brine: Duan-Sun (2003) extended to NaCl/CaCl2 mixtures with methane and nitrogen impurities
  • Residual trapping: Land (1968) snap-off model with Killough (1976) capillary hysteresis
  • Geomechanics: Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion, Hubbert-Willis (1957) fracture pressure, Byerlee (1978) friction
  • Dissolution: Duan-Sun solubility budget with Neufeld et al. (2010) convective mixing enhancement
  • Mineral trapping: Lasaga (1984) kinetic precipitation (active from year 20 onward)

Use your registered email and password. The app creates a local project database in your browser automatically.

2

Create a new project

3

Select jurisdiction

Define stratigraphic zones, faults, and grid resolution in the Geology panel (Stage 1).

Click "Run Simulation" in the Simulation panel. Results appear across all downstream panels automatically.

Section 4

Geological Model Builder

The Geological Model Builder constructs the 3D stratigraphic and structural framework that the simulation grid is built from. It operates through three tabs: Stratigraphy, Faults, and Property Preview.

[Image #4]
Geology panel showing the three tabs (Stratigraphy, Faults, Property Preview) with the stratigraphy tab active and two zones defined

4.1 Stratigraphic Zones

Zones are defined from top to bottom. The uppermost zone should be the caprock seal, followed by the injection reservoir intervals. Each zone requires:

  • Name and lithology: Sandstone, shale, carbonate, or evaporite/salt
  • Top depth (m) and thickness (m): Depths are in metres below surface
  • Porosity mean and standard deviation: Spatially correlated variation is applied via fractional Brownian motion noise
  • kH (horizontal permeability, mD) and kV/kH ratio: Controls Darcy flow rate and buoyancy-driven vertical migration
  • Capillary entry pressure (MPa): Sealing capacity of the zone
  • Wettability: Water-wet (default for most sandstones), mixed, or oil-wet (certain carbonates)
  • Active for injection flag: Only zones with this enabled receive CO₂ from injection wells
  • Is caprock flag: Zones with this flag act as the impermeable seal and are coloured grey in the 3D viewer

Tip: Always define at least one caprock zone (isCaprock = true) above the injection zone. Without a caprock, the simulation has no structural trap and CO₂ will migrate upward indefinitely.

[Image #5]
Stratigraphic zone editor showing a two-zone model: 50m shale caprock and 150m sandstone reservoir with porosity and permeability inputs

4.2 Fault Definition

Faults are added in the Faults tab. Each fault definition controls both the 3D visual representation (semi-transparent plane in the viewer) and the flow physics (transmissibility multipliers on cell faces). Parameters include:

  • Position X/Y (0-1): Normalised model coordinates. (0.5, 0.5) = model centre
  • Strike (degrees 0-360): Horizontal orientation of the fault trace
  • Dip (degrees 0-90): Inclination from horizontal; 90 = vertical fault
  • Throw (m): Vertical displacement across the fault plane
  • Length (m): Along-strike extent of the fault
  • Sealing Factor (0-1): 0 = perfectly sealing (transmissibility = 0), 1 = fully open (transmissibility = 1)
  • Clay Smear Factor (0-1): Additional sealing from clay gouge. Effective sealing = max(sealingFactor, claySmearFactor)
  • Fault Zone Thickness (m): Controls how many grid cells the fault intersects

In the 3D viewer, sealing faults (sealingFactor less than 0.3) are shown as red semi-transparent planes; open faults (greater than 0.7) as green; partially sealing as amber. A fault trace line marks the intersection with the top surface.

[Image #6]
Fault definition panel showing two faults: one sealing (red, sealingFactor 0.1) and one partially open (amber, sealingFactor 0.5) with strike/dip inputs
[Image #7]

4.3 Grid Resolution and Non-Uniform Spacing

Grid dimensions are set as Nx x Ny x Nz (default 60 x 60 x 20 = 72,000 cells, maximum 100 x 100 x 30 = 300,000 cells). Higher resolutions increase accuracy at the cost of simulation time.

Non-uniform Cartesian spacing v2.0 is applied automatically. A cosine-bell refinement function generates cells that are up to 3x finer near the grid centre (where injection wells are typically located) and coarser at domain boundaries. This is equivalent to near-well adaptive mesh refinement without increasing the total cell count or changing the solver topology.

Non-uniform spacing improves near-well BHP accuracy, injection saturation gradients, and dissolution rate estimation. The spatial variation in cell width is visible in Grid View as narrower columns near the centre and wider columns at the boundary.

The Property Preview tab shows the geological grid coloured by lithology, permeability, or fault transmissibility before any simulation is run. This is useful for verifying that zone boundaries, fault intersections, and permeability heterogeneity are as intended.

Section 5

Formation and Well Properties

The Formation panel (Stage 2) defines the quantitative reservoir parameters used in all physics calculations. It is organised into 6 steps accessed via a step wizard.

5.1 Reservoir Dimensions and Rock Properties

  • Depth to reservoir top (m): Minimum 800 m for supercritical CO₂ storage (P greater than 7.38 MPa required)
  • Total thickness (m): Gross formation thickness including all zones
  • Area (km²): Lateral footprint; controls total pore volume
  • Net-to-Gross ratio: Fraction of gross thickness that is porous reservoir (vs. interbedded shales)
  • Geometry type: Anticline, dome, fault, layered, stratigraphic, channel, or uploaded gridfile
  • Porosity (fraction) and permeability (mD): Formation-average values used in capacity and injectivity calculations
  • Dykstra-Parsons Vdp coefficient (0-0.85): Permeability variation; 0 = homogeneous, 0.85 = highly heterogeneous
  • kH/kV anisotropy ratio: Ratio of horizontal to vertical permeability (greater than or equal to 1)
  • Connate water saturation Swi: Irreducible water saturation (default 0.15)
[Image #10]
Formation panel Step 1 showing reservoir dimension inputs (depth, thickness, area) with a live capacity estimate updating as values change

5.2 Fluid Conditions

  • Initial pressure (MPa): Hydrostatic pressure at reservoir depth; auto-computes from depth if left blank
  • Temperature (°C): Formation-average reservoir temperature (overridden by geothermal gradient when set)
  • Monovalent salinity (mol/kg): NaCl equivalent concentration; reduces CO₂ solubility
  • Bivalent salinity (mol/kg): CaCl₂ equivalent; affects Duan-Sun solubility calculation
  • Salt type: NaCl, CaCl₂, or Mixed
  • Methane and nitrogen fractions: CO₂ stream impurities that affect Span-Wagner density via impurity mixing rules

5.3 Geothermal Gradient v2.0

Instead of manually computing and entering reservoir temperature, you can specify the geothermal gradient. CarbonLens then auto-derives the temperature at each relevant depth.

  • Surface Reference Temperature (°C): Typical values: 10-15°C (temperate regions), 25-30°C (tropical/equatorial). Default 15°C.
  • Geothermal Gradient (°C per 100m): Typical values: 2.5 (stable cratons), 3.0 (Southeast Asia, North Sea), 3.5 (tectonically active regions), 4.0+ (volcanic margins)

When both fields are set, a live display shows the computed reservoir midpoint temperature. The PVT engine uses a per-pressure-point temperature estimate derived from hydrostatic depth, replacing the previous single isothermal assumption. The Simulation HUD shows the temperature range from reservoir top to base.

Example: For a formation at 2000m depth in Peninsular Malaysia with surface T = 27°C and gradient = 3.0°C/100m: reservoir midpoint T = 27 + 3.0 x 2000/100 = 87°C. This is entered automatically once the two gradient parameters are set.

[Image #11]
Geothermal gradient input fields showing surface reference temperature (27°C), gradient (3.0°C/100m), and the live-computed reservoir midpoint temperature (87°C)

5.4 Geomechanical Parameters

  • Caprock friction angle (degrees) and cohesion (MPa): Mohr-Coulomb strength parameters for the sealing formation
  • Biot coefficient (0-1): Poroelastic coupling between pore pressure and effective stress (default 0.7 for sandstone)
  • Poisson ratio: Lateral strain response (default 0.30)
  • Overburden gradient (MPa/m): Vertical stress gradient (default 0.023 MPa/m)
  • Stress ratio K0 (sigma-h / sigma-v): Horizontal-to-vertical stress ratio; extensional basins ~0.55-0.65, compressional ~0.90-1.20
  • Young's modulus (GPa): Controls the surface heave calculation (sandstone ~5 GPa, carbonate ~15-30 GPa)
  • Fractured reservoir flag: Applies a 0.20x fracture compliance correction to the heave estimate
  • Lithology class: Sandstone or carbonate; governs the mineral trapping kinetic rate constant

5.5 Well Configuration

Up to 8 injection wells can be defined. Each well requires:

  • Position (X, Z): Normalised scene coordinates -1 to 1. (0, 0) = model centre
  • Injection rate (Mt/year): Mass of CO₂ injected per year at plateau
  • Label: Descriptive name shown in the 3D viewer
  • Ramp-up years: Number of years to linearly increase from 0 to full rate
  • Ramp-down years: Number of years to linearly decrease from full rate to 0 before final shut-in

During simulation, the Peaceman (1978) log-radial injection weighting distributes the injection volume across cells within the injection radius, concentrating CO₂ near the wellbore to produce physically realistic near-well saturation profiles.

[Image #12]
Well configuration panel showing two wells with different positions, injection rates, and ramp schedules, plus the well positions visible in the 3D canvas preview

5.6 Perforation Intervals v2.0

By default, CO₂ is injected into the lower 40% of each active reservoir zone (typical for buoyancy-driven storage in saline aquifers). For more precise control, each well can specify custom perforation intervals.

Each perforation interval defines:

  • Top fraction (0.0-1.0): 0.0 = reservoir top, 1.0 = reservoir base
  • Bottom fraction (0.0-1.0): Must be greater than top fraction
  • Flow fraction (%): Percentage of the well's total injection rate assigned to this interval

All flow fractions across a well's perforation intervals must sum to 100%. The UI displays a validation warning if they do not. Multiple intervals allow modelling zone-selective injection strategies common in thick carbonate reservoirs and in multi-zone saline aquifer sequences.

Example: A 200m thick formation with a high-permeability basal zone. Define two intervals: 0.0-0.5 (top half, 20% flow) and 0.5-1.0 (basal zone, 80% flow). CO₂ is preferentially placed in the more permeable zone for better injectivity.

[Image #13]

Section 6

Fluid Properties Panel

The Fluid Properties panel is a read-only computed panel showing in-situ CO₂ and brine properties at the specified reservoir temperature and pressure. Values update automatically when formation parameters change. No inputs are required here.

  • CO₂ density (kg/m³): From Span-Wagner (1996) EOS at reservoir T and P
  • CO₂ viscosity (Pa.s): From Fenghour et al. (1998)
  • Brine density (kg/m³): Garcia (2001) mixing rule for CO₂-saturated brine
  • CO₂ solubility (mol/kg): Duan-Sun (2003) at reservoir salinity
  • Diffusion coefficient (m²/s): Molecular diffusion in brine
  • Interfacial tension (mN/m): CO₂-brine IFT; relevant to capillary entry pressure
  • Phase state: Supercritical (T and P both above critical point) or subcritical (gas phase; storage efficiency significantly reduced)

Section 7

Running Simulations

The Simulation panel controls the full solver chain. Simulation runs natively in the browser with no server calls. A typical 50-year project at 60x60x20 resolution completes in 2-5 seconds.

[Image #15]
Simulation panel showing the Run Simulation button, project duration input, animation speed selector, IMPES toggle, and the simulation status indicator showing "Running..."

7.1 Simulation Controls

  • Project Duration (years): Total injection and post-injection monitoring period (10-500 years)
  • Run Simulation: Executes the full solver chain and generates all results
  • Pause / Resume: Pauses the animation at the current timestep
  • Stop: Halts a running simulation
  • Animation Speed (1x, 2x, 5x, 10x): Controls playback speed of the CO₂ plume animation in the 3D viewer
  • Reset: Clears all results and returns to the pre-simulation state

The solver chain executes in sequence: storage screening, DOE capacity calculation (P10/P50/P90), pressure field computation, PVT field statistics, geomechanical assessment, plume grid simulation, and post-injection gravity current (Hesse et al. 2008) if all wells have shut in.

7.2 IMPES Solver Mode v2.0

CarbonLens provides two time integration schemes selectable via the IMPES toggle in the Simulation panel:

Table 2 - Solver Comparison
SchemeDefaultCFL constraintBest for
Explicit forward-EulerYesYes (CFL-adaptive sub-stepping)k less than 500 mD, standard grids
IMPES (Gauss-Seidel)NoNo (60-iteration pressure solve per year)k greater than 500 mD, fine grids, high injection rates

IMPES (Implicit Pressure, Explicit Saturation) solves the pressure field implicitly via 60 Gauss-Seidel iterations per timestep, removing the pressure CFL stability constraint. The saturation update then proceeds explicitly using the pressure-derived fluxes.

When to use IMPES: Enable it when simulating high-permeability formations (k greater than 500 mD), fine grids (greater than 80x80x25), or high injection rates where the explicit scheme may require many sub-steps and become slow.

Section 8

3D Reservoir Viewer

The 3D Reservoir Viewer is the central visual workspace. It displays the reservoir as either a smooth mesh or a cellular grid, with live CO₂ plume animation, HUD overlays, and multiple colour property modes.

8.1 Grid View vs Smooth Mesh View

The view mode is toggled with the Grid/Mesh button in the top-right of the 3D canvas:

  • Smooth Mesh View: Shows the reservoir as a continuous geometric surface (anticline, dome, fault trap, etc.). Best for visualising structural geometry and geological context.
  • Grid View: Reveals the simulation as discrete coloured cells. Required for the Simulation HUD overlays, cell-level property inspection, and visualising the CO₂ plume at cell resolution. Non-uniform cell spacing is visible here.

8.3 Simulation HUD Panels

When Grid View is active after a simulation has been run, five overlay panels appear in the bottom-left of the 3D canvas. They update live as the animation plays.

Year Counter

Shows the current simulation year versus total project duration, with a teal-to-blue progress bar. The timestep slider at the top centre of the canvas allows jumping to any year.

CO₂ Phase Distribution

A stacked bar showing the breakdown of stored CO₂ mass across the four trapping mechanisms at the current timestep: Structural (caprock), Residual (Land snap-off), Dissolved (Duan-Sun), and Mineral (Lasaga kinetic). Percentages are displayed in a legend below the bar.

Cell Legend

Colour codes for cell states in CO₂ phase view: Brine (dark navy), Structural Trapping (red), Residual (green), Dissolved (teal), Mineral (amber), Caprock (grey), Imbibition front (purple).

In-situ CO₂ Density Panel v2.0

Reports spatial CO₂ density variation from the Span-Wagner PVT lookup table evaluated at each pressure field point:

  • Min density (kg/m³): At the plume edge where pressure is lowest; indicates subcritical risk
  • Mean density (kg/m³): Pressure-field-weighted average; compared to baseline single-point estimate
  • Max density (kg/m³): Near the injection well at peak pressure
  • Capacity adjustment (%): Ratio of true field-average density to baseline; positive = denser than assumed (more mass per pore volume)
  • Temperature range: When geothermal gradient is set, shows T at reservoir top to T at reservoir base
  • Subcritical warning: A yellow badge appears when any pressure field point is below 7.38 MPa (CO₂ critical pressure), indicating possible gas-phase CO₂ with significantly reduced storage efficiency

Wellbore BHP Panel v2.0

  • Peaceman BHP (MPa): Reservoir-side Darcy pressure from log-radial well model (Peaceman 1978)
  • Hydrostatic BHP (MPa): WHP (10 MPa default) + CO₂ column weight = WHP + rho x g x depth / 1e6
  • Tubing friction delta-P (MPa): Darcy-Weisbach single-phase CO₂ friction in 4-inch nominal tubing, computed from total injection rate using the Blasius correlation
  • Fracture margin (MPa): Fracture pressure minus the higher of Peaceman BHP and total arrival BHP; positive (green) = safe; negative (red) = fracture risk

Fracture risk warning: When the fracture margin is negative, a red warning badge appears: "BHP exceeds fracture pressure. Reduce injection rate or WHP." Reduce the per-well injection rate or increase formation thickness to resolve this.

Geomechanical Safety Panel

Only shown when geomechanics data is available:

  • Mohr-Coulomb Safety Factor: Colour-coded: greater than 2.0 = green (safe), 1.5-2.0 = light green, 1.2-1.5 = yellow (caution), 1.0-1.2 = orange (warning), less than 1.0 = red (failure)
  • MAIP margin (MPa): Headroom between current injection pressure and the Maximum Allowable Injection Pressure
  • Seismicity risk: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH based on Coulomb failure function change on optimally oriented faults

Section 9

Geomechanics Panel

The Geomechanics panel provides a detailed breakdown of formation integrity and induced seismicity risk. Results are computed automatically after each simulation run from the formation's stress state and the pressure field.

  • Caprock stress (MPa) and fracture pressure (MPa): Vertical effective stress on the caprock and the pressure at which hydraulic fracturing initiates
  • Mohr-Coulomb Safety Factor: The ratio of available shear strength to applied shear stress
  • MAIP and MAIP margin (MPa): Maximum Allowable Injection Pressure and headroom to fracture
  • Fault slip potential (dCFF, MPa): Coulomb Failure Function change on optimally oriented faults; positive = increased failure probability
  • Surface heave (mm): Estimated poroelastic surface uplift from pore pressure increase (Young's modulus and Biot coefficient dependent)
  • Induced seismicity risk: Low / Moderate / High classification
  • Friction angle, cohesion, Biot coefficient, and overburden stress are also displayed for transparency
[Image #22]

Section 10

Leakage Risk Panel

The Leakage Risk panel provides a probabilistic assessment of CO₂ leakage pathways through and around the caprock seal. It draws on the simulation result, geomechanical assessment, and formation properties.

  • Caprock integrity score: Combined assessment of caprock thickness, entry pressure, and fracture margin
  • Area of Review (AoR): Radius within which pressure perturbations may affect existing wells or surface features (corrected for formation heterogeneity when Vdp is set)
  • Leakage probability: Probabilistic estimate from Monte Carlo analysis of key uncertainties
  • Risk category: Low / Moderate / High with jurisdiction-specific threshold values
  • Recommended monitoring programme: Suggested monitoring technologies based on risk level and injection volume

Section 11

Screening and Site Ranking

The Screening panel evaluates the site against a 12-criterion technical suitability matrix aligned with DOE/NETL best practices and the CarbonSAFE screening methodology.

  • Criteria include: depth (supercritical CO₂ requirement), formation temperature, permeability, caprock integrity, salinity, area, overpressure risk, and geomechanical safety
  • Each criterion returns Pass / Marginal / Fail with the actual value vs threshold
  • A weighted suitability score (0-100) summarises overall site quality
  • DOE P10/P50/P90 storage capacity estimates are displayed with the full formula breakdown
  • The site is benchmarked against the 20 validated formations in the CarbonLens database

Section 12

Monte Carlo Uncertainty Quantification

The Monte Carlo panel runs 500 simulation realisations over user-defined parameter distributions to characterise storage capacity uncertainty. Results feed the P10/P50/P90 estimates used in permit applications.

  • Sampled parameters: Porosity, permeability, depth, thickness, and area can each be assigned a distribution (uniform or normal with mean and standard deviation)
  • Output: Cumulative distribution function of storage capacity (Mt)
  • Tornado chart: Ranks parameters by their contribution to total uncertainty variance
  • P10/P50/P90 extraction: Capacity percentiles extracted directly from the empirical CDF
[Image #25]

Section 13

History Matching

The History Matching panel enables calibration of the simulation against field monitoring observations. This is used when injection has already begun and monitoring data (seismic-derived plume radii, gravimetric CO₂ mass estimates) are available.

1

Import observations

Upload a CSV file or manually enter observed plume radius (m) and injected CO₂ mass (Mt) at each year of available monitoring data.

2

Run inversion

CarbonLens uses the Boait (2012) analytical inversion to back-calculate a calibrated effective permeability k_eff from the observed plume radius at a reference year.

3

Review fit quality

The residual plot shows simulated vs observed plume radius over time. RMSE and R² are computed. Acceptable match: RMSE less than 20% of the mean observed radius.

4

Lock calibrated parameters

Accept the k_eff value. It is stored in the formation parameters and used for all subsequent forward projections.

[Image #26]
History matching panel showing observation import table, residual plot with simulated (dashed) and observed (dots) plume radius vs year, RMSE display, and the calibrated k_eff value
Section 14

Economics Panel

The Economics panel computes project financial metrics based on the simulated storage volume and user-defined cost and revenue inputs.

  • CO₂ credit price (USD/tonne): Market or regulated price for CO₂ storage credits
  • Capital cost (MUSD): Upfront investment (wells, compression, surface facilities)
  • Operating cost (USD/tonne CO₂ injected/year): Annual operating expenditure per unit of CO₂ stored
  • Discount rate (%): For NPV calculation
  • NPV (MUSD), IRR (%), and payback period (years): Computed over the project life
  • Sensitivity chart: NPV vs CO₂ price over a range showing breakeven price

Section 16

Export and Permits

The Export panel produces all documentation outputs from the simulation results. Four export types are available:

1

HTML Validation Report

A comprehensive multi-section pre-feasibility report including formation parameters, simulation outputs (capacity, trapping breakdown, plume dimensions), geomechanical assessment, screening matrix, and an explicit validation conclusion. Section 17

Registry

The Registry panel provides persistent project storage, loading, and sharing. Projects are saved to the browser's local IndexedDB database and optionally shared via a public link.

  • Save project: Saves all formation parameters, well configuration, simulation results, and geomechanics to the local database
  • Load project: Restores a saved project and all its results without re-running the simulation
  • Visibility toggle: Private (default) or Shared. Shared projects are listed in the public registry and accessible to other users
  • Tags: Add classification tags (e.g. "saline aquifer", "Malaysia", "validated") for filtering
  • Project comparison: Select multiple projects from the list to compare P50 capacity, screening score, and geomechanical safety factor side by side
  • Delete: Permanently removes a project from the local database

Section 18

Validation Reports

CarbonLens has been validated against 20 published storage sites across 5 continents. All validation reports are publicly accessible without signing in at validation/index.html.

Sleipner-Utsira: Primary Validation Benchmark

CarbonLens achieves 100% alignment with 20 years of Sleipner monitoring data across 7 key metrics (plume radius, CO₂ density, injection pressure, solubility trapping fraction, containment probability, geomechanical safety factor, and residual trapping fraction) within accepted scientific tolerance for a screening-level model.

Each validation report contains: the formation parameters used, CarbonLens simulation outputs, a side-by-side comparison table with published field data, and an explicit validation conclusion with pass/marginal/fail ratings. The full methodology is documented in the Technical White Paper (Section 5).

Section 19

Glossary

AoRArea of Review: the zone within which pressure perturbations from injection may affect existing infrastructure or create leakage pathways
BHPBottomhole Pressure: the pressure at the wellbore at reservoir depth, combining hydrostatic column, tubing friction, and Darcy drawdown
CFLCourant-Friedrichs-Lewy stability condition: the timestep limit for explicit finite-difference solvers based on the ratio of cell size to flow velocity
Critical point (CO₂)The thermodynamic state above which CO₂ is supercritical: temperature greater than 31.1°C and pressure greater than 7.38 MPa
EOSEquation of State: a thermodynamic relationship used to compute fluid density and viscosity from temperature and pressure
IMPESImplicit Pressure, Explicit Saturation: a time integration scheme where the pressure equation is solved implicitly (removing the CFL pressure constraint) and saturation is updated explicitly from the result
k_effEffective permeability: the calibrated permeability value back-calculated from field monitoring data using the Boait (2012) plume inversion, used to correct for sub-grid heterogeneity
MAIPMaximum Allowable Injection Pressure: the injection pressure above which hydraulic fracturing of the caprock or reservoir may initiate
mDMillidarcy: unit of permeability. 1 mD is approximately 10^-15 m²
Mohr-Coulomb SFSafety Factor from Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion: ratio of available shear strength (cohesion + normal stress x tan(friction angle)) to applied shear stress
MtMegatonne: 10^9 kg. Unit for CO₂ storage capacity and injection rate
Peaceman BHPReservoir-side wellbore pressure calculated using the Peaceman (1978) log-radial Darcy model, accounting for radial pressure drawdown around the injection well
PVTPressure-Volume-Temperature: the characterisation of fluid properties (density, viscosity, compressibility) as a function of in-situ reservoir conditions
Residual trappingCapillary trapping of CO₂ as disconnected ganglia in pore throats during brine imbibition, described by the Land (1968) snap-off model
Structural trappingCO₂ accumulation beneath an impermeable caprock seal, analogous to oil and gas trap geometry
Supercritical CO₂CO₂ above its critical temperature (31.1°C) and pressure (7.38 MPa), exhibiting liquid-like density (600-900 kg/m³) and gas-like viscosity. Target state for efficient geological storage.
Transmissibility multiplierA scaling factor (0-1) applied to the flow transmissibility between two adjacent grid cells to represent the effect of a fault or low-permeability barrier without explicitly gridding the fault geometry
VE solverVertically Integrated (VE) solver: a 2D gravity current model that collapses the 3D plume into a depth-averaged representation, following Nordbotten and Celia (2006)
VdpDykstra-Parsons coefficient: a dimensionless measure of permeability variation (0 = perfectly homogeneous, 0.85 = highly heterogeneous). Used to apply Shook and Mitchell (2009) sweep efficiency corrections.
WHPWellhead Pressure: the pressure at the surface injection point before the hydrostatic CO₂ column adds weight during descent to reservoir depth. Default 10 MPa in CarbonLens.
